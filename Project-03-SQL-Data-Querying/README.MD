# Project 3: SQL & Data Querying

This project was completed as part of the **AnalystLab Africa Data Analytics Internship**. The project focuses on applying SQL to relational and transactional datasets to perform data analysis, answer business questions, identify patterns, and optimise query performance.

The project uses two datasets:

1. **Chinook Database (Music Store Data)**
2. **Sales Data Sample**

---

## 1. Chinook Database — Music Store Data

The Chinook database was analysed using MySQL to explore customer distribution, purchasing behaviour, revenue, and customer value.

### Analysis Performed

- Filtered and sorted customer records using `WHERE` and `ORDER BY`.
- Analysed customer distribution by country using `GROUP BY` and `COUNT()`.
- Used `HAVING` to identify countries with more than four customers.
- Calculated total revenue and average invoice value using `SUM()` and `AVG()`.
- Used `INNER JOIN` to connect customers with their invoice records and analyse total customer spending.
- Used `LEFT JOIN` to identify customers without purchase records.
- Applied subqueries to calculate average customer spending and identify customers spending above the average.
- Used `ROW_NUMBER()` and `RANK()` to rank invoices by transaction value.
- Used `PARTITION BY` to rank invoices independently for each customer.
- Identified each customer's highest-value invoice.
- Created an index on `customerid` as part of query optimisation.

### Key Insights

- The **USA has the largest customer base with 13 customers**, followed by **Canada with 8 customers**, while **France and Brazil each have 5 customers**.
- Customers are distributed across **24 countries**, indicating a geographically diverse customer base.
- **Helena Holý** was identified as the highest-spending customer, with total purchases of **$49.62**.
- The analysis identified **no customers without invoice records**, indicating that every customer in the dataset had made at least one purchase.
- Average customer spending was approximately **$39.47**, with **22 customers spending above this average**.
- Window functions were used to identify and rank high-value invoices and analyse individual customer purchasing behaviour.

## 2. Sales Data Analysis

The Sales Data dataset was analysed using SQL Server to investigate product performance, revenue trends, customer purchasing behaviour, and geographical markets.

### Analysis Performed

- Examined the structure and size of the imported sales dataset.
- Used `SELECT`, `WHERE`, and `ORDER BY` to filter and sort sales records.
- Analysed revenue and order performance by product line using `GROUP BY`.
- Used `HAVING` to identify product lines generating more than $100,000 in revenue.
- Calculated the highest and lowest sales values using `MAX()` and `MIN()`.
- Used subqueries to analyse customer spending.
- Applied `RANK()` with `PARTITION BY` to identify the top two product lines for each year.
- Used window functions to calculate monthly running revenue totals.
- Applied `ROW_NUMBER()` to analyse customers' largest orders.
- Identified top-performing product lines based on units sold and revenue.
- Analysed quarterly revenue trends over time.
- Examined customer purchasing behaviour and repeat purchases.
- Compared performance across different deal sizes.
- Identified the top five countries by revenue and customer activity.
- Applied indexing concepts to frequently grouped and filtered columns to improve query performance.

### Key Insights

- The **USA dominates sales by country**, generating **$3,627,982.83 from 35 customers**, more than three times the revenue generated by **Spain ($1,215,686.92)**. Spain's revenue, however, is largely driven by the **Euro Shopping Channel** account.
- **Classic Cars is the dominant product line**, generating **$3,919,615.66 in revenue**, more than double the **Vintage Cars** category at **$1,903,150.84**.
- Classic Cars ranked **#1 in every year analysed (2003, 2004, and 2005)**, confirmed using the `RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY Year_ID ...)` window function.
- **91 out of 92 customers are repeat buyers**, indicating strong customer retention and a business model largely driven by recurring customer relationships.
- **Euro Shopping Channel ($912,294.11)** and **Mini Gifts Distributors Ltd. ($654,858.06)** together contribute approximately **21% of total company revenue**, highlighting a potential customer concentration risk.
- The analysis of quarterly and monthly revenue provided insight into how sales performance changed over time and supported the identification of revenue trends.
- Product line, country, and customer-level analysis demonstrated how SQL can be used to identify key revenue drivers and support business decision-making.
